# A Simple Way to Remember Water Breaks During a Busy Workday

When your attention is on a deadline, a meeting, or a row of browser tabs, small needs can slip out of view. If you often realize late in the day that you have not paused for water, the answer may not be a stricter rule. A reminder attached to a useful moment in your schedule can make the intention easier to notice.

## Choose a cue you will actually see

Start by noticing when water breaks tend to disappear. Is it during back-to-back meetings? When you are concentrating on one long task? In the hour after lunch? Pick a cue that already belongs to your workday, such as the end of a call or the start of an afternoon block. A reminder tied to a real transition is easier to understand than a vague instruction to “drink more.”

## Put it where it fits

Open your planner and choose a time that leaves room to respond. If the morning is full, do not place reminders in the middle of every call. Try one after a meeting or before you begin a new work block. You can use a recurring routine on selected days, then change the timing when your schedule shifts.

## Keep the routine personal

There is no universal reminder schedule that suits everyone. Your day, preferences, and circumstances are your own. If you have been given specific guidance about fluids, follow that guidance rather than using a general productivity tip. FlowWell is a reminder tool; it does not set water targets or provide health advice.

Make the reminder easy to act on. Keep a glass or bottle where you work if that is convenient. If you work away from your desk, choose a time when you are likely to be nearby. The point is to create a small, practical cue, not to interrupt every moment of concentration.

## Adjust instead of giving up

After a few days, check whether the chosen time worked. If it repeatedly appears during a call, move it. If you notice the reminder but are not ready, skip it and consider a better cue. A plan is allowed to change as your calendar changes.

At day’s end, FlowWell’s summary can show which planned moments you completed. Use that record as feedback about the schedule, not as a judgment. If a reminder is useful, keep it. If it adds noise, reduce it.

**A practical starting point:** choose one water break after a meeting you have most workdays. Set a time that gives you a little breathing room, try it for a week, and adjust based on your actual routine.
